👋About Me

Where business meets the data

I sit at the intersection of commercial thinking, analytics, and business intelligence, and I care most about the decision the data is meant to support.

I am a commercial and business intelligence analyst with a strong quantitative foundation and a few years of experience across data and customer facing roles. The thread running through my work is simple. Data only matters when it leads to a decision, so that is where I start.

Most problems are not really data problems. They are business questions waiting for a clear answer. I begin by understanding what someone is actually trying to decide, then work backwards to the data that answers it. That habit means my analysis gets used, not filed away.

On the technical side, I work across the full workflow. I write SQL to extract and shape data, use Python to clean and manipulate complex datasets, and build dashboards in Power BI and Tableau that turn numbers into something a non technical person can act on. I enjoy the part many people avoid, tracking down the inconsistency, understanding why it happened, and making the data something people can trust.

On the commercial side, working directly with customers and operational teams has taught me to listen, ask better questions, and connect every piece of analysis back to a real outcome, whether that is cost, efficiency, retention, or revenue.

What makes this work in any sector is that the core skill is not the industry, it is the thinking. Structured problem solving, clean data, and clear communication apply just as well to oil logistics as they do to retail, housing, or finance. I have moved across different industries already, and each time the value has come from asking the right questions and turning messy information into something useful.

⚙️How I Work

From a business question to a trusted answer

A structured, repeatable approach I follow on every project, built to keep work aligned, accurate, and genuinely useful.

1

Understand the question

Before opening a single file, I sit down with whoever needs the answer and ask what decision they are trying to make. A request for a sales report usually means something more specific, so I dig until I know exactly what they need and why.

2

Scope and document

I map out where the data lives, which tables and fields I will need, and what the finished output should look like. Then I agree a realistic deadline, so there are no surprises later for me or for them.

3

Extract and shape

The data is almost never in one place. I write SQL to pull it from different tables and join it together, then use Python to clean up the messy parts and reshape it into a single, usable dataset.

4

Clean and validate

Before I trust a number, I check it. I strip out duplicates, fix missing or inconsistent entries, and run quick statistical checks to catch anything that looks off, then sense check my totals against what the business already knows.

5

Analyse and model

This is where the question gets answered. I look for the patterns, trends, and relationships that explain what is actually happening, then build the result into a clean Power BI or Tableau model that updates as new data comes in.

6

Tell the story

Insight that no one understands changes nothing. I turn the analysis into a clear narrative and practical recommendations, shaped around the audience, and hand it over with documentation others can build on.
